An artificial neural network is a computational network of connected units. Each unit receives weighted signals from other units, combines those inputs, applies an activation function, and passes its output to connected units. During training, an artificial neural network adjusts connection weights so its predictions better match examples. The network's behavior depends on both the units and the pattern of connections between them.

This structure gives researchers a precise model for studying how signals move through a neural network. It also creates a careful point of comparison with biological neural networks: artificial units and weights are mathematical abstractions, while biological neurons communicate through electrical activity and chemical synapses. Both are networks in which connections influence the flow of activity, but their components and learning mechanisms are different.
