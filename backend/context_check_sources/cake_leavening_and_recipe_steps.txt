Cake texture depends on how ingredients are combined and how gas is produced and retained before the crumb sets. Baking powder releases carbon dioxide when moistened and heated; baking soda requires an acidic ingredient to react effectively. Creaming butter and sugar can incorporate air, while eggs help bind the batter. Excess mixing after flour is added can make some cakes tougher, and opening the oven early can let an unset cake collapse.

To test a cake recipe, measure ingredients consistently, preheat the oven, and prepare the pan before mixing. Combine dry ingredients separately, cream butter and sugar, add eggs gradually, then mix in the dry ingredients and liquid just until combined. Transfer the batter to the pan and bake until the center springs back or a tester comes out with a few moist crumbs. Change one variable at a time, such as leavener amount or oven temperature, and note the resulting height and crumb.
