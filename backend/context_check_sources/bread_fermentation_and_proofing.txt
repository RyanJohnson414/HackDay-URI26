Bread dough changes during fermentation as yeast consumes available sugars and releases carbon dioxide. The gas forms bubbles that are held by the hydrated, kneaded flour proteins, so the dough expands while it proofs. A baker judges proofing by the dough's volume and feel rather than by the clock alone, because room temperature, flour, and yeast activity affect how quickly it rises. In the oven, expanding gas and water vapor contribute to oven spring before the loaf structure sets.

For a basic loaf, mix flour, water, yeast, and salt until no dry flour remains. Knead until the dough becomes elastic, cover it, and let it rise until visibly expanded. Shape the dough, allow a second proof, then bake in a fully preheated oven until the crust is browned and the center is cooked. Record the ingredient amounts, dough temperature, proof time, and baking time when comparing batches.
