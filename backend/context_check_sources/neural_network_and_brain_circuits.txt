A biological neural network is made of neurons connected by synapses. Activity in one neuron can affect other neurons through those connections, and a brain circuit combines signals from many connected cells. The strength and timing of synaptic signals help shape how activity travels through a neural network. Learning can involve lasting changes in these connections, a process studied as synaptic plasticity.

Artificial neural networks also contain connected units, weighted connections, and changing patterns of activity. Their connection weights are updated by mathematical training rules such as backpropagation; these weights are not literal synapses, and artificial units are not biological neurons. Comparing the two kinds of neural network helps clarify how connectivity and signal flow shape learning while keeping the biological and computational mechanisms distinct.
