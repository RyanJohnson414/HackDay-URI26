Fish schooling and artificial neural networks are different systems, but both can be studied as networks of interacting units. A fish uses local sensory input from nearby fish to adjust its speed and direction. In an artificial neural network, a unit uses weighted input from connected units to update its activation. In each case, local interactions can contribute to a larger group pattern or network output.

The comparison is about network organization and information flow, not identical behavior. Fish are living animals with sensory and motor systems; artificial neural networks perform numerical calculations and may learn by adjusting weights from prediction error. Studying both systems can help researchers ask how local signals, connectivity, and feedback shape collective behavior and computation.
